## Claim

The live AAR practice that people cite as working is four questions: intend, did, why, adapt-next-time. A lesson body that is only a claim plus caveats is missing the trigger (when to apply) and the adaptation (what to do differently).

## Supporting Evidence

> **Source [EXPERT]:** Wharton 2021: four key questions — What did we intend? What did we do? Why did it happen that way? What will we do to adapt / how do we repeat success? (content_hash:0d960c49), retrieved 2026-08-17
>
> **Source [PRIMARY]:** GAO-01-1015R quotes NASA: a lesson must be significant, valid, and applicable — identifies a specific design, process, or decision that reduces failures or reinforces a positive result. (content_hash:e659b4ed), retrieved 2026-08-17

## Disconfirmation Search

NASA defines a lesson as significant, valid, and applicable (identifies a specific design/process/decision). That is closer to a recommendation than to four questions, and it still failed at retrieval. So the four-question shape is not sufficient. It is a better write format than unstructured prose, not a substitute for retrieval.
