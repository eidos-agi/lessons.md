## Claim

The scarce step is applying a lesson to the next piece of work, not writing it down. Systems that only measure capture will look full and still repeat mistakes.

## Supporting Evidence

> **Source [PRIMARY]:** GAO-01-1015R: 17.5% of respondents applied a lesson identified through LLIS; 23% submitted a lesson in two years; 43% submitted none. Mentoring was the first-choice improvement. (content_hash:e659b4ed), retrieved 2026-08-17
>
> **Source [EXPERT]:** Wharton 2021 / Senge: going through the motions of an AAR is easy; putting AARs into DNA is the challenge. Four questions end in what will we do to adapt for a better outcome. (content_hash:0d960c49), retrieved 2026-08-17

## Disconfirmation Search

Looked for orgs where capture volume predicted fewer repeats. GAO: only 17.5% of NASA managers had applied a lesson identified through LLIS; 23% had submitted one in two years. Wharton/Senge argue the living practice (discussion + next-time adaptation) is what fails when AARs become a form. Counter: Huber posts AARs to a DB with a Chairman award — that is a capture incentive, not an application metric. I found no source that treats 'number of lessons filed' as the outcome that matters.
