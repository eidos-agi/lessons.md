## Claim

A searchable store of lessons does not change behavior unless the right lesson is retrieved at the moment of the next decision. Capture-without-retrieval is the dominant failure mode.

## Supporting Evidence

> **Source [PRIMARY]:** GAO-01-1015R Survey of NASA Lessons Learned Process (2001): 58% of managers said processes do not allow them to retrieve the right lessons at the right time; 53% found lessons useful <25% of the time; LLIS was not a primary source vs reviews and informal discussion. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-01-1015R/html/GAOREPORTS-GAO-01-1015R.htm (content_hash:e659b4ed), retrieved 2026-08-17
>
> **Source [EXPERT]:** Wharton Executive Education, After-Action Reviews (July 2021, Henshaw). Quotes Senge: corporate AAR efforts fail when reduced to a sterile technique. Lists four live questions (intend / did / why / adapt). Also the disconfirming Huber/Microsoft searchable-DB stories. https://executiveeducation.wharton.upenn.edu/thought-leadership/wharton-at-work/2021/07/after-action-reviews-simple-tool/ (content_hash:0d960c49), retrieved 2026-08-17

## Disconfirmation Search

Searched for evidence that lesson databases work as pull systems. Wharton (2021) reports J.M. Huber and Microsoft post AARs to searchable intranet DBs and treat that as success. NASA still operates a public LLIS. Those are existence proofs that some organizations keep a store. They do not show retrieval-at-decision-time: Huber's story is employees can search; NASA managers told GAO they mostly do not. I did not find a controlled study showing a pull-only lesson DB reduced repeat mistakes. The claim is not databases are useless; it is a store that is not forced into the next decision does not reliably change behavior.
